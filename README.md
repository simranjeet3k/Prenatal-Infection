Question 4- Two PRIORITY nursing interventions and Two RELEVANT medical interventions
Two priority nursing interventions- 
1.	Symptom management and monitoring
It’s important to help Liza bring her fever down in a safe way by encouraging her to stay well-hydrated and, if needed, take acetaminophen, which is safe in pregnancy. She should have her temperature checked regularly and be aware of danger signs like a very high fever, chills, rash, or severe pain. Rest and good nutrition will also support her recovery. Keeping track of her baby’s movements is another key part of monitoring. This is a priority because fever can be risky for both mother and baby, and managing symptoms early helps prevent complications and keeps them both safe (Egloff et al., 2020).
Reference- Egloff, C., Sibiude, J., Couffignal, C., Mandelbrot, L., & Picone, O. (2020). Causes and consequences of fever during pregnancy: A retrospective study in a gynaecological emergency department. Journal of gynecology, obstetrics and human reproduction, 49(9), 101899. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jogoh.2020.101899

 
2.	Community Resource Connection
We will link Liza to newcomer services, prenatal classes, or cultural health groups in Edmonton. This can reduce isolation and improve her confidence in managing pregnancy. Moreover, it can build support networks and reduce stress, which improves maternal and fetal health.
Two relevant medical interventions-
1.	Diagnostic intervention- On looking at Lizza’s symptoms, proper diagnostic tests should be performed to find the cause of the fever and discomfort. It looks like she might have UTI, so urinalysis and blood count should be performed to look for any infection. Fever can be a reason to go to the emergency consultation during pregnancy, as it can be related to a fetal adverse outcome. Egloff et al. (2020) say that more accurate diagnostic and prognostic methods could be developed to help reduce unnecessary hospitalizations and antibiotic use, lowering healthcare costs, while ensuring closer monitoring when there is a higher risk of complications related to fetal infection because of fever. Once the cause is identified (bacterial vs. viral), start appropriate antibiotic / antiviral if indicated. Use pregnancy-safe antibiotics.
Reference- Egloff, C., Sibiude, J., Couffignal, C., Mandelbrot, L., & Picone, O. (2020). Causes and consequences of fever during pregnancy: A retrospective study in a gynaecological emergency department. Journal of gynecology, obstetrics and human reproduction, 49(9), 101899. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jogoh.2020.101899
2.	Use of Safe Antipyretic and Supportive Care
Acetaminophen can be given to help bring down Liza’s fever, but only at the lowest effective dose since it’s the safest option during pregnancy. Along with medication, she should stay well-hydrated by drinking fluids and rest as much as possible. If she becomes severely dehydrated or is unable to keep fluids down, IV fluids and electrolyte checks may be necessary. Should her fever remain high or her condition worsen, an urgent review by her obstetric team or hospital admission would be important to keep both her and her baby safe (Egloff et al., 2020) 
Reference- Egloff, C., Sibiude, J., Couffignal, C., Mandelbrot, L., & Picone, O. (2020). Causes and consequences of fever during pregnancy: A retrospective study in a gynaecological emergency department. Journal of gynecology, obstetrics and human reproduction, 49(9), 101899. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jogoh.2020.101899

Question 7. Three PRIORITY teachings in lay terms
1.	“Wash your hands often and avoid contact with people who are sick.”
Proper handwashing is very important, especially when a person is pregnant. It helps remove germs that may be picked up from surfaces, food, or other people. During pregnancy, the body’s immune system works differently, which makes it easier to catch infections, and that might be the reason Liza is feeling feverish and discomfort. We would teach Liza to stay away from sick people who have a cold, flu, or other contagious illness to reduce her chances of getting sick. Hand washing regularly will not only help her but also help protect the baby, since some illnesses can pass to the baby during pregnancy. By doing proper and regular hand hygiene, she can avoid any further complications (Nalule et al., 2021). 
Reference- Nalule, Y., Buxton, H., Ir, P., Leang, S., Macintyre, A., Pors, P., Samol, C., & Dreibelbis, R. (2021). Hand hygiene during facility-based childbirth in Cambodia: a theory-driven, mixed-methods observational study. BMC Pregnancy & Childbirth, 21(1), 1–14. https://doi-org.bowvalley.idm.oclc.org/10.1186/s12884-021-03901-7

2. “Cook meat well and avoid unpasteurized dairy or undercooked foods.”
Khaled et al. (2024) explained that during pregnancy, women are encouraged to eat foods like milk, yogurt, eggs, chicken, beef, goat meat, and small fish because they provide protein, calcium, and iron to support the mother and baby’s growth. As Liza has just immigrated here, we would teach her to eat home-cooked food because it’s safer during pregnancy. When she prepares meals at home, she can make sure meat, eggs, and dairy are fully cooked and fruits and vegetables are washed properly, which helps prevent infections that could harm her or her baby. Home-cooked meals are also easier to manage, more affordable, and allow her to include familiar foods while keeping her diet healthy. She can eat green leafy vegetables, sweet pumpkin, and fruits such as apples, oranges, bananas, and sometimes papaya, which add essential vitamins and fiber for healthy development. To stay safe, she should avoid unpasteurized dairy or juice.
